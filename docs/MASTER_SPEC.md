# Fantasy Football Decision Engine — Master Model Specification & Roadmap

*Version: canonical schema v3 / SQLite schema v1 / historical research dataset schema v2 | 23 October 2026 | Status: M0, M1, and M2.1–M2.6 complete and accepted; M3.1 implemented, review pending*

This is the single authoritative living specification, implementation guide, and milestone tracker. It synthesizes the 76 agreed design decisions into a coherent description of the intended model; the older decision log is archived for historical reference only. Status labels and evidence statements distinguish implemented and verified behavior from planned or unresolved behavior; a prospective requirement is not evidence that an algorithm or ESPN field has been implemented or confirmed.

## 1. Purpose, outcomes, and product boundaries

### 1.1 North-star objective

Use ESPN’s player-specific future weekly fantasy point projections, real league rules, and the current league state to estimate the distribution of season outcomes and determine which roster actions maximize expected championship probability. The engine evaluates every manager, not just the user, and supports interpretation of effects on playoff qualification, seeding, future waiver competition, and final placements. Secondary decision objectives may be supported later, but championship probability is the default optimization objective.

The engine is not initially intended to outperform ESPN’s forecasts of individual player mean points. Rather, it converts their projections into realistic uncertain seasons, accounts for actual roster constraints and decision consequences, and compares actionable roster changes against the appropriate baseline. ESPN is the sole initial projection source. External inputs may be considered later only if they demonstrate validated incremental value and are approved as part of a versioned official model.

### 1.2 Design principles

Build clean-sheet architecture; the prior Streamlit/ESPN/trade/waiver code is optional reference material and must not dictate design. Deliver an end-to-end, structurally complete system early, with simple provisional methods behind swappable interfaces, then replace individual calculations after validation. Prefer correctness, empirical evidence, reproducibility, and speed; use configurable assumptions instead of silently inventing unsupported rules. Keep the model independent of its presentation layer.

The first release is operated from the repository/CLI, not a new dashboard. It initially supports one ESPN league while using league_id and season keys to permit future multi-league capabilities. The eventual UI exposes one officially approved model rather than a confusing list of experimental versions. No ongoing hosted service or automated ESPN polling is required for V1.

Project-wide fail-fast engineering policy (agreed): Favor explicit invariants, transparent failures, and diagnostic traceability over silently forgiving unexpected states. Do NOT introduce automatic fallback rules, catch-and-continue behavior, guessed tie priority, substitute data, implicit correction of invalid rosters, defaulted missing league rules, or other safety-net logic unless the relevant behavior has been explicitly designed, approved, documented and tested. When an expectation is violated, report a meaningful exception/validation failure with the offending trade step, team/player IDs, snapshot/config values and violated invariant, and do not produce authoritative results from an unverified state. Observed invariants such as 'at most one participant needs a drop for a given two-party trade' must be asserted or checked, NOT achieved by hardcoding codepaths that exclude the unexpected possibility before it can be observed. Add negative and adversarial tests specifically designed to reveal invariant violations. Distinguish supported, intentionally designed alternative algorithms (such as user-provided league settings for fields ESPN does not expose, calibrated statistical pooling, or explicit operational recovery checkpoints) from silent error-masking fallbacks. Clearly marked partial Monte Carlo results produced at an approved resource cap are expected behavior, not an error-handling excuse. Fail fast on structural/data violations, while allowing only documented normal probabilistic variation and intentionally configurable settings. The objective is discoverable bugs and auditable model integrity, not needless crashes or lack of informative diagnostics.

System-wide exhaustive-search principle (confirmed): Every action/search family—including single trades, multi-transaction chains, standalone waivers, combined trade/waiver moves, streaming, and any future transaction type—must be capable of enumerating ALL candidates within an explicitly defined finite user-selected universe, subject ONLY to real legality/structural feasibility and explicitly approved model constraints. Do not hardcode attractiveness thresholds, positive intermediate V(R), positive counterparty value, minimum expected championship delta, search-rank cutoffs, fixed top-N limits, favored partners, or implicit candidate caps as eligibility requirements. Make narrowing controls independent, explicit and configurable: teams/players, transaction shape, chain length, pickup/drop scope, V(R) screening, counterparty acceptance proxies, numerical budget, and practical resource constraints. Default heuristics/presets may improve speed but must be independently disableable and must disclose any coverage lost. An exhaustive mode bypasses all optional pruning and guarantees full feasible-candidate coverage of the chosen universe; this is distinct from evaluating every candidate to a particular statistical precision. If computation or time caps prevent finishing enumeration or Monte Carlo evaluations, mark precise stage coverage, candidates not examined, and incomplete statistical convergence; never silently label a partial search exhaustive. Use a shared abstraction across action families to ensure consistent semantics and tests. Fail fast on unapproved implicit filtering or unexpected feasibility assumptions.

### 1.3 V1 boundaries and deferred behaviors

V1 begins only at fully completed fantasy scoring-week boundaries: settled past weeks remain actual, future unstarted weeks are simulated, and partial/live-week forecasts are deferred. Managers choose optimal projected legal starters; future discretionary trades, speculative waiver upgrades, manager inattention and intentionally suboptimal starters are not modeled in baseline. Only essential future waiver acquisitions are simulated when a required starter slot cannot otherwise be filled. Player performances are conditionally independent in V1; independent future injury events and game/player correlations are deferred. Results are not claims that actual managers will behave optimally or that injury uncertainty is captured exhaustively.

## 2. System architecture and execution lifecycle

### 2.1 Component boundaries

A. ESPN Adapter discovers current and sample historical data and converts it to validated canonical records. B. LeagueSnapshot represents immutable as-of rosters, rules, standings, schedule, player-week projections/eligibility, and shared free-agent pool. C. PlayerOutcomeModel transforms ESPN future weekly projections into sampled point outcomes. D. WeeklyLeagueEngine executes essential waivers, projected-optimal lineups, matchups, standings and playoff/consolation transitions. E. RosterValuePolicy and ScenarioCompleter transform user-directed hypothetical changes into coherent full-roster states. F. ScenarioEvaluator pairs baseline and scenario runs with matched random shocks. G. CandidateGenerator searches possible trades, waivers and action sequences, adding feasibility filters. H. ResultsAggregator, Persistence and Reporter produce cumulative output, uncertainty/diagnostics, temporary recovery checkpoints and future UI-ready results.

Component interfaces must be explicit enough to test and replace actual model policies, but not a heavyweight abstract plugin framework. ESPN IDs, fantasy weeks, eligibility matrices, scenario ownership and snapshot metadata remain consistent across all modules. Data preparation and reporting are ordinary readable Python; performance-sensitive simulation internals favor compact arrays, compiled loops and batched execution.

### 2.2 Normal workflow

Explicit data refresh → stage/validate and atomically publish immutable ESPN snapshot → choose existing validated snapshot (with age warning if stale) → select official model and fast/detailed run configuration → evaluate baseline season trajectories → optionally apply a structurally coherent hypothetical roster state or generate feasible candidate moves → rerun on the SAME snapshot using paired shocks → report probability deltas, uncertainty, transaction dynamics and league-wide consequences.

Simulation execution never silently triggers ESPN retrieval. Baseline and scenarios in one comparison must use exactly the same input snapshot. A convenience command may explicitly orchestrate a refresh followed by simulation, but the two steps remain separately identifiable. Neither an old valid snapshot nor a model version change should alter an already archived original forecast.

### 2.3 Immutable state and provenance

Every run records league_id, season, snapshot ID and collection time, first simulated week, model component versions, official configuration, random seed/path definition, performance mode/precision, source completeness indicators and code/build version. Past actual results and future projections are distinguishable by fantasy week and as-of time. Historical replays remain labeled separately from original prospective forecasts.

## 3. ESPN data integration, audit and storage

### 3.1 The initial ESPN audit (first work item)

Before depending on API assumptions, build a read-only diagnostic against the actual current league through espn-api and, as needed, inspected underlying ESPN endpoints. The audit tests data availability and actual semantics for league identity, scoring, roster sizes/slots and positional eligibility, teams and current ownership, real free agents, week-specific projections and actual fantasy points, statuses/byes, regular-season matchups/standings/points-for/tiebreaks, current and evolving waiver rules, and the exact championship and consolation bracket formats. Capture endpoint/attribute, data type, granularity, availability, completeness, sample sanitized value, retrieval parameters, reliability, and simulator use.

Classify every needed field as confirmed, obtainable by extra request/processing, derivable, unknown, or unavailable; separate critical blockers from noncritical warnings. Reconcile counts and identity constraints, and document every gap and feasible mitigation. This is a bounded evidence-based audit, not the construction of a complete historical data warehouse.

M0.2 verified the bounded connectivity subset against the configured 2026 league using `espn-api` 1.0.1 plus its read-only raw league request. League/team identity, manager IDs, standings records, current roster ownership, player IDs/eligibility, scoring items and raw configured roster slots were available. The live run reconciled 14 teams and 233 uniquely owned rostered players. Negative player IDs are valid for team defenses. The library's derived `position_slot_counts` is unreliable for sparse ESPN slot IDs, so later schema work must use validated raw slot IDs. Full sanitized provenance, validation evidence and deferred questions are recorded in `docs/ESPN_CONNECTIVITY.md`; projections/free agents remain M0.3 and waiver/tiebreak/postseason semantics remain M0.4.

M0.4 verified 16 ordinary roster places plus one separate IR slot, traditional non-FAAB waivers with a complete current priority order and weekly-reset evidence, explicit trade controls, H2H standings fields with `TOTAL_POINTS_SCORED` as the configured first seeding tiebreak, and a six-team non-reseeded championship with two byes and 1/1/2-week rounds. Completed 2024 and 2025 league responses independently reconstruct championship entrants, seed pairings, winner/loser transitions, placement games, and two-week score aggregation. These are current-league findings, not validation constants. League discovery must dynamically parse actual ESPN settings and distinguish valid-and-understood, valid-but-unsupported/unverified, and invalid/contradictory data; no team count, roster, IR/flex, division, playoff, round, or consolation structure is universal. Exact multiple-claim processing, raw `NONE` matchup-tie semantics, and the current eight-team consolation ladder remain unresolved and must be verified or supplied as explicit conflict-checked league configuration before authoritative simulation. Full provenance and the rule matrix are in `docs/ESPN_LEAGUE_RULES_AUDIT.md`.

### 3.2 Representative historical ESPN data

Investigate a small sample of previous seasons, especially matched historical player-week projected and actual fantasy points. Verify whether each retrieved historical projection really represents the pregame value available at that time or a later restatement; do not conflate them. Evaluate coverage by season, week, position, and player, including missingness, projection zeros and selection bias. Also inspect historical rosters/ownership, schedules and league-rule changes as feasible. If true as-of projections are missing, record that limitation: do not train an ostensibly historical error distribution using leaked retrospective forecasts. ESPN-only sourcing remains the initial policy; a broader external dataset is a possible validated enhancement, not an automatic fallback.

M0.5 sampled ESPN weeks 1, 8, 14, and 17 in both 2024 and 2025. The raw season endpoints returned 8,760 player-weeks, 7,489 projection values, 5,214 actual values, and 5,000 structurally matched pairs. Weekly projections are raw `statSourceId=1`, `statSplitTypeId=1` records; actuals use source 0 with the same weekly split. Missing values and 4,294 explicit projection zeros were preserved separately, and no operational effective-zero policy was applied. No investigated record exposed authenticated creation, revision, or original as-of timing. Repeated retrieval and a second ESPN view established only present-day response consistency. Consequently every returned projection remains historical-with-pregame-provenance-unverified, with zero verified-pregame observations. Forecast-horizon vintages are unsupported by the investigated source, and current historical scoring results can carry retroactive-change risk. M3 must not promote this material to a genuine pregame calibration sample without independent timestamped corroboration; prospective immutable ESPN snapshots are the recommended ESPN-only path if separately approved. Detailed evidence is in `docs/ESPN_HISTORICAL_PROJECTION_AUDIT.md`.

Post-M0.5 approval: these unverified historical projection/actual pairs may be used for explicitly provisional exploratory calibration, but they must retain their evidence label and must never be described as authenticated pregame observations. Provisional Monte Carlo probability estimates are permitted before empirical forecast calibration is independently validated, provided every output distinguishes structural/model correctness, provisional statistical calibration, and empirically validated statistical calibration. M0.6 does not select a distribution family, fit parameters, or define statistical promotion thresholds. Scheduled prospective collection, a historical forecast warehouse, third-party projections, and forecast-horizon research are deferred enhancements rather than prerequisites to M1 or V1.

### 3.3 Canonical league snapshot

Include team and player identities; complete real current rosters/lineup capacity and positional eligibility; an unrostered ESPN player pool and ownership map; current settled standings and PF; future schedule; regular-season and postseason format; current waiver order/rules; each player’s projection for each future week; status/byes/missingness; league scoring settings and source timestamps. Maintain no more than one owner per player per simulation path. Missing future projections remain explicitly distinct from confirmed ESPN zeros in canonical data. Store the nullable raw projection, a `missing` / `explicit_zero` / `present` status, the derived effective projection, and its source. Under the approved V1 policy, a missing raw projection produces `effective_projection=0` with source `missing_projection_policy`; an explicit ESPN zero produces the same effective value with source `espn`. Never overwrite the raw missing value or misattribute an imputation to ESPN. Evidence and methodology are recorded in `docs/MISSING_PROJECTION_POLICY.md`.

M1.1 implements schema version 2 as frozen, slotted, ESPN-independent dataclasses with tuple collections and stable ID joins. Schema v2's targeted amendment separates the exact observed player-eligibility slot namespace from explicitly configured lineup-slot capacities; an observed unconfigured category is preserved but cannot be occupied. `RosterEntry` is the only authoritative ownership relationship; recognized `ONTEAM`, `FREEAGENT`, and `WAIVERS` states reconcile against it while unknown raw acquisition enums remain preserved. `PlayerWeek` uses `(player_id, season, scoring_week)` and preserves finite raw values exactly: negative and positive present values remain unchanged with ESPN provenance, explicit zero remains ESPN zero, and missing derives policy zero. Projected-lineup availability is derived as `effective_projection > 0`. The snapshot stores explicit first/final simulation weeks, full intervening player-week coverage, cumulative present standings, dynamic rules, calendar-only regular/postseason matchup periods, per-matchup bracket classifications and explicit scheduled/bye/partial/placeholder states, NFL games/byes, source references, evidence interpretations, and unresolved gaps. Championship, consolation, placement, and unresolved entries can coexist in one postseason period without fabricated participants. Rule evidence supports validated immutable nested JSON values as well as scalars. Pure validation separates canonical contradictions from unresolved capability-specific meanings. Deterministic schema-v2 JSON rejects incompatible versions and round-trips slot namespaces, nulls, zeros, signed IDs, UTC timestamps, raw enums, structured rule evidence, and gaps. M1.1 does not implement persistence, publication, or simulation. The contract is documented in `docs/CANONICAL_MODEL.md`.

M1.2 implements sequential read-only retrieval, in-memory raw source capture/digests, pure ESPN-to-canonical translation, bounded transient retries, player-pool completeness checks, current-versus-future source precedence, critical mutable-state reconciliation, sanitized diagnostics, and audited YAML rule-gap configuration. The approved schema-v2 amendment preserves raw eligibility slot 25 in a separate observed namespace without inventing capacity. The repeated 17 October 2026 live acceptance run passed for explicit fully unplayed weeks 8–18, with 14 teams, 1,052 players, 11,572 player-weeks, 49 fantasy matchups, the complete NFL universe, critical-state reconciliation, and deterministic serialization. Details are in `docs/ESPN_CANONICAL_ADAPTER.md`.

### 3.4 Snapshot publication and staleness

Fetch into temporary staging; validate critical schema, coverage and internal invariants; only then atomically mark a new immutable snapshot simulation-ready. A half-fetched, malformed or wrong-league refresh must fail and leave the last good snapshot untouched. Snapshot validators should be strict about actual missing core league data, not blindly treat every unprojected individual player as a catastrophic refresh failure. Record failure diagnostics and quality metrics.

Snapshot age is displayed with clear stale-data warnings, but valid snapshots remain runnable for reproducibility and historical comparisons. Missing or corrupt core data is a blocking error, not a stale-data warning. M1.4 adds a deterministic report outside the canonical document: structural violations still raise precise validation errors, while ordinary missing projections, explicit zeros, finite negatives, unrostered players, and observed unconfigured eligibility categories remain aggregate information. Reports calculate total and week/position/ownership projection coverage from canonical records and disclose unresolved rule capabilities without treating snapshot validity as simulator capability readiness. Refresh and simulation are separate commands. V1 does not require scheduled background ESPN harvesting; if later useful for research, a lightweight independent scheduled collection task can be built with explicit uptime, credential and retention arrangements.

M1.5 implements read-only snapshot listing, selection, inspection, and verification. Compact listings are deterministically newest-first and may filter by league and season without loading canonical objects or raw response payloads. Selection uses an exact UUID when supplied; otherwise both league and season are required to choose the newest matching snapshot. A missing or corrupt explicit UUID never falls back. Inspection performs one fully verified load, reuses M1.4 quality reporting, calculates informational age from retrieval completion, and keeps summary output sanitized. Teams, rosters, players, projections, rules, schedules, and canonical source-request metadata require explicit detail selection; raw captured response bodies are never printed. Snapshot-specific verification remains separate from explicit database-wide SQLite/foreign-key checks. These operations neither refresh ESPN nor mutate stored state.

### 3.5 Persistence and security

Use lightweight local SQLite as V1's authoritative persistent store. The complete deterministic versioned canonical JSON document is the sole authoritative normalized snapshot representation; no parallel relational team/player/player-week model exists. New publications use canonical schema v3; historical v2 documents remain immutable and readable. SQLite schema v1 stores append-only snapshot metadata, the canonical document bytes and checksum, plus gzip-compressed deterministic JSON representations of every captured ESPN response with request provenance and uncompressed checksums. Each publication receives a new UUID unrelated to content. Retrieval completes before one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction atomically inserts all records; failure before commit rolls back the candidate and never alters earlier snapshots. A subsequent verification failure identifies the already committed UUID and never claims rollback or deletes/retries it. Published rows reject updates and deletes, all successful refreshes are retained indefinitely by default, and newest selection uses publication UTC time then snapshot UUID. Routine reads verify the requested snapshot's database/document versions, identities, canonical and raw checksums, references, decompression, deserialization, and canonical validation without an unconditional database-wide scan; explicit integrity diagnostics run both SQLite integrity and foreign-key checks. SQLite uses WAL with `synchronous=FULL` and foreign keys on every repository connection. Sensitive ESPN cookies/credentials are never stored in request metadata; the database remains a private ignored local artifact. Do not introduce Parquet storage, scheduled collection, or completed-scenario caching in V1. Full details are in `docs/SNAPSHOT_STORAGE.md`.

### 3.6 ESPN-authoritative league rules and configuration portability

Agreed architecture principle: ESPN's current league configuration is the authoritative source for every retrievable rule, not a template to copy into hardcoded fantasy assumptions. During M0, exhaustively inventory which rules, settings and bracket metadata ESPN actually exposes, their authoritative endpoints, schema and interpretations. During each deliberate league refresh, ingest and validate the configured scoring categories/values, roster slots/capacity/eligibility, lineup lock and transaction constraints as relevant, waiver priority/claim behavior, tiebreak rules, regular-season schedule and standings methodology, playoff qualifiers/byes/brackets/round durations and consolation structure. Derive all league mechanics from this versioned, immutable LeagueRules snapshot used by the simulator; a different league or season with a different supported ESPN configuration should work without source-code edits. Avoid assuming this league's current 14-team/6-playoff/two-week-final structure is globally true. Treat rule changes between snapshots as meaningful input changes that require validation and a new reproducible simulation baseline; never retroactively mutate a previously simulated snapshot.

If a required behavior is not exposed by ESPN or is ambiguous, do not silently invent a generic rule. Record an explicit 'unknown/unverified' state, seek an official ESPN settings page or faithful observed historical bracket/transaction evidence, and allow a clearly labeled, provenance-tracked manual league-specific configuration ONLY as a last resort for an otherwise implementable rule. Critical unresolved mechanics block authoritative results until resolved; noncritical gaps can be disclosed with a justified provisional assumption for research runs. Build adapter-level verification tests against actual ESPN behavior where feasible, and fail loudly when unsupported setting types or schema changes are encountered rather than quietly applying stale defaults. This policy supersedes any illustrative inverse-standings waiver order, PF tiebreak or fixed postseason mapping previously mentioned as if universal.

League-level fallback configuration (agreed): Any league-level setting unavailable through the verified ESPN API/endpoints must be represented as an explicit, human-editable per-league configuration value (for example a YAML league config), never hardcoded in simulator logic or scattered across source files. The ingestion layer merges ESPN-exposed values with these fallback values into one validated and versioned LeagueRules snapshot. Precedence is ESPN first for fields it exposes reliably; local config fills absent fields rather than silently overriding known ESPN settings. Record each effective value's source (ESPN vs configured fallback), audit/verification status and retrieval/config timestamp. Provide a concise, documented schema, permitted enumerations and validation errors that tell the user exactly which config key is missing or invalid; keep the common case low-friction so switching leagues or updating rules requires config changes rather than code edits. Preserve the resolved configuration with every run for reproducibility. Surface conflicts between ESPN values and fallback config explicitly; do not quietly accept inconsistent rules. This applies to scoring, transactions, standings, roster, postseason and other relevant league mechanics as API coverage dictates, not just tiebreakers. Explicit experimental rule overrides, if supported later, must be separately opt-in, visually distinguished and never masquerade as actual ESPN league settings.

## 4. Player performance and uncertainty model

### 4.1 Means and conditional availability

For each unstarted fantasy week, use the latest available ESPN player-specific weekly fantasy point projection as the mean input, with no independent momentum or injury-adjusted forecast of expected points. In V1, a positive effective projection means eligible/available for that week and an effective projection of zero means unavailable. A missing raw projection retains its explicit `missing` quality status but, under the approved conservative policy, derives an effective zero; a confirmed ESPN zero retains `explicit_zero`. Reassess week by week. ESPN designation alone does not override this heuristic in V1. Do not use simulated realized points or later developments to decide the pre-week lineup or whether a waiver claim should occur. Do not introduce a positional-average, injury-return, replacement-level, or other projection fallback for missing values.

V1 uses the ESPN projection available at snapshot retrieval for every future player-week and does not forecast subsequent revisions to ESPN's published number. Treat the expected adjustment from a future ESPN revision as zero solely as an approved simplifying assumption, not as an empirically validated random-walk property. Apply no forecast-horizon-dependent expected-point adjustment or variance multiplier in V1. Preserve the snapshot time so later runs remain reproducible even if ESPN changes its projection.

### 4.2 Outcome distribution research

Draw each player-week’s realized fantasy score from an empirically evaluated uncertainty distribution conditional on ESPN projection; the exact form is UNRESOLVED pending the historical-data audit. Candidate approaches include empirical projection-error resampling and suitable fitted distributions; evaluate by position, projection magnitude, zeros, support (e.g., unrealistic negatives), tails, calibration, sharpness and out-of-sample scoring. Avoid assuming homogeneous errors across QBs, RBs, WRs, TEs, kickers and defenses. The approach should be simple enough to sample cheaply at scale and sufficiently expressive to capture material downside/upside asymmetry.

V1 treats individual player outcomes as independent conditional on their projections. That is a practical simplifying assumption, not a scientific claim about real football. Later extensions may add game/team correlation, performance uncertainty changes around injury, and a separate injury/availability stochastic process if empirical evidence supports an improvement.

Distribution-family selection (confirmed): Keep empirical conditional projection-error resampling, suitable parametric distributions, and defensible hybrid/smoothed approaches as competing candidates; no method is preselected for V1. Compare each with temporally held-out ESPN projections and realized scores, stratifying calibration diagnostics by position and continuous projected-point level. Assess bias, coverage, log/CRPS or other appropriate proper scores, sharpness, discrete zero mass, permissible negative outcomes, lower and upper tails, stability under sparse data, and runtime at full Monte Carlo scale. Where raw resampling has sparse conditional support, test smoothing or partial pooling; where parametric methods misrepresent asymmetry, zeros or tails, reject or amend them. Prefer the simplest robust method that measurably performs best under these criteria, with an explicit documented fallback and reproducible validation report. The data audit first establishes which genuine as-of ESPN records exist; final distribution form is a later evidence-based M3 decision, not an assumption made in design discussions.

### 4.3 Projection-horizon uncertainty (research direction)

A separate potential enhancement to the Monte Carlo player-outcome model is to expand its predictive variance as the number of weeks between an ESPN forecast snapshot and the target game increases, while leaving the ESPN expected points unchanged. Conceptually, use a horizon-conditional scale factor on forecast errors, estimated from honest as-of ESPN pregame and multiweek-ahead snapshots: sigma(position, projection level, lead time) or an empirically estimated variance-ratio by forecast horizon. This reflects possible degradation of longer-horizon forecast precision; do not assume monotonic growth, a fixed percentage per week, or that variance is the only forecast property that changes. Empirically test whether the errors are actually more dispersed at longer lead times and whether calibration/sharpness improves. Important: a wider player-performance distribution does not mean its expected points are discounted; nonlinear championship probabilities can change despite unchanged means. V1 should initially use the best defensible common residual model unless reliable as-of lead-time-labeled data supports a more complex variance structure. The earlier V1 choice to model projection >0 as available and not simulate additional future injuries remains a separate limitation: variance inflation alone cannot substitute for actual missing games or structural availability risk. If historical snapshot lead times are unavailable, defer this enhancement or test it prospectively rather than invent a curve.

### 4.4 Position and projection-level uncertainty; horizon if observable

Calibration requirement (agreed): investigate and compare the distribution of ESPN projected-versus-actual fantasy scores conditioned on (i) player position (including QB, RB, WR, TE, K and D/ST as appropriate), (ii) the magnitude of the ESPN point projection (for example, low projected scores such as 3 versus higher values such as 20), and (iii) forecast lead time from the authentic as-of projection snapshot to the game. The conditional mean, residual spread, skewness, probability of zero/near-zero realized scores, upper/lower tails and calibration may differ. Do not presume proportional or constant absolute errors: a three-point projection and a twenty-point projection need not share the same absolute standard deviation, coefficient of variation, or distribution family. Investigate joint interactions rather than assuming three independent correction factors; whether or not horizon variance expands remains an empirical question. Treat projection=0 in the current V1 league model as unavailable, and missing forecasts as separate from low but positive projections.

Implementation selection and overfit control: start with exploratory positional and projection-range summaries, including empirical sample counts and honest pregame timestamps; compare pooled, position-conditioned, projection-level-conditioned and combined/partially pooled models using held-out forecasts and proper probabilistic scoring/calibration diagnostics. Point buckets are primarily exploratory strata, not necessarily discontinuous hard thresholds in production—continuous smooth functions of the ESPN mean, hierarchical shrinkage or minimum-sample fallbacks may be more stable. Avoid overfitting thin position × point-level × horizon cells. Favor the simplest validated model with defensible performance, practical sampling speed and realistic support/tails, and use a documented fallback when historical horizons or group sizes are inadequate. Preserve the ESPN expected-point inputs as the forecasting basis; uncertainty calibration must not secretly substitute third-party player projections. Specific bins, sample thresholds, distribution families and interaction structure remain research decisions after the ESPN historical data audit.

### 4.5 Projection-error representation and data-availability gate

Agreed research approach: compare absolute residuals (actual fantasy points minus ESPN projected points), relative errors/ratios (actual divided by projected where safely defined), and alternative conditional predictive models rather than choosing an error scale by intuition. Evaluate how each representation handles very small and zero ESPN projections, negative fantasy outcomes where allowed by scoring rules, heavy tails, asymmetry, heteroskedasticity and differences across positions/projection levels. Ratios may explode at small positive projections and are undefined when projected points equal zero; never silently apply a ratio to zero or conflate missing forecasts with explicit zeros. Compare held-out predictive calibration, proper scores, tail realism and computational sampling cost. Continuous/smoothed conditioning on ESPN point expectation is preferable to arbitrarily discontinuous hard bucket cutoffs when data supports it; buckets may still be used for descriptive diagnostics. Preserve ESPN point projections as the starting means rather than adopting untested systematic changes to expected points.

Data feasibility distinction: ESPN historical records may expose only a pregame projection for each player-week, not the full sequence of forecasts made several weeks before the game. This is UNVERIFIED until the M0 audit. If only pregame values are available, proceed with robust calibration of position- and projected-score-dependent uncertainty using those genuinely as-of observations, while treating forecast-horizon variance changes as unidentifiable from that historical sample. Do not approximate long-lead forecasts with post-hoc pregame projections or fabricate a horizon multiplier. Revisit horizon effects only if authenticated older snapshot revisions, prospective as-of collection, or another later-approved evidentiary source becomes available. Horizon calibration is useful but not a blocker for the initial Monte Carlo uncertainty model.

V1 zero-outcome modeling (agreed): For a player whose ESPN forecast is greater than zero and who is therefore pre-week lineup-eligible, sample a full realistic realized-score distribution that can include exactly zero or near-zero fantasy points and extreme downside when supported by authentic historical projection-to-actual outcomes. Such zero outcomes may reflect poor games, limited play or unanticipated in-game injuries; the model need not identify the cause in V1. Do not truncate positive-projection player outcomes to be strictly positive or artificially prevent zero-point realizations. Distinguish this stochastic score outcome from the deterministic V1 pre-week availability decision: an explicit ESPN projection of exactly zero rules the player out of that week's projected lineup, while missing projections remain a separate data-quality state. Do not add an independent injury-absence sampler in V1, since it would potentially double count zero/downside risk already included in the empirical performance distribution. Later, a separately validated availability-versus-conditional-performance decomposition may improve realism, but any extension must preserve correct unconditional scoring probabilities and avoid double counting. Evaluate conditional zero frequencies by position and projection level and use proper discrete mass where empirically indicated, not an arbitrary universal zero rate.

### 4.6 Validated projection-bias correction: simulated outcomes only

Approved design (subject to empirical validation): Maintain two explicitly distinct projection quantities. (1) Raw ESPN weekly projected fantasy points remain immutable and are the ONLY basis for V1 projected-optimal lineup choices, reactive essential waiver-add ranking, deterministic roster completion and the projected-point replacement-adjusted V(R) heuristic. (2) The PlayerOutcomeModel may apply a versioned, empirically justified conditional bias adjustment to the distribution of REALIZED player-week points sampled by Monte Carlo, based on position, projected level and other supported conditioning variables; e.g., a raw ESPN projection of 9 may correspond to an empirically calibrated distribution centered around 8. Apply the correction when drawing realized scores after roster actions and lineups are fixed, not as a preprocessing rewrite of ESPN projection fields or automatic rescaling of every model decision input. The championship/placement probabilities naturally reflect calibrated sampled results. Bias corrections must pass relevant held-out/out-of-sample tests and be promoted to the official model under Section 11; no correction is assumed just because an in-sample mean differs. Record both raw and calibrated outcome expectations as distinct provenance fields when needed, including their model version, without mutating source data.

Interpretation: ESPN-projection-based lineup and waiver policies may cease to be optimal under the calibrated means (e.g., a 9-point ESPN player may be chosen over an 8.5-point ESPN player although their calibrated expected scores reverse). This is an intentional V1 policy distinction, not an implementation bug: simulate choices based on original ESPN information and assess their true calibrated consequences. Evaluating alternate decision policies using corrected means is a separately validated future experiment, not a consequence of adopting a sampling correction. Corrected-score uncertainty and empirical downside/zero mass must remain coherent; do not accidentally apply the bias adjustment twice.

Bias-correction shrinkage policy (agreed): Estimated systematic ESPN forecast biases should be regularized/shrunk toward no correction when data are sparse or uncertain. The degree of shrinkage should depend on the strength of evidence, effective sample size, instability across seasons and groups, and held-out predictive results, not a fixed arbitrary percentage. Consider hierarchical/partially pooled position- and projection-level estimates, smooth regularized corrections, or other statistically principled methods; do not adopt a hard bucket-by-bucket raw average error as the automatic adjustment. Corrections may approach the full supported estimate when evidence is strong, but do not promise elimination of all measured bias or introduce a change without meaningful out-of-sample improvements. Ensure testing respects as-of ESPN projections, temporal splits and model-selection leakage. Maintain raw ESPN projections for all decision policies; apply only the approved calibrated/shrunk adjustment to simulated player outcomes, not twice or upstream. Exact shrinkage method, pooling structure and evidence thresholds remain part of the empirical distribution-model selection workstream.

V1 player-specific uncertainty policy (agreed): Do not add individual-player volatility parameters or player-specific score-error distributions. Conditional performance and correction distributions are group-level functions of position, raw ESPN projected point level and any other separately validated shared covariates; individual players with the same modeled covariates use the same conditional distribution. This avoids unstable estimates from short, nonstationary player histories and protects the initial model from false precision. Future research enhancement (post-V1, explicitly on roadmap): evaluate player-level residual volatility and distribution shape using sufficiently long, as-of, multi-season ESPN projection/actual histories, accounting for changing usage, team, injuries, role and sample selection. Require hierarchical shrinkage toward group estimates, minimum effective data, temporal validation, and an out-of-sample improvement in distribution calibration and decision outcomes before adoption. A group-level zero/downside tail remains allowed for all players; lack of player-specific parameters does not mean identical outcomes for all players regardless of projection or position.

V1 dependence assumption (confirmed): Sample all player-week realized scores independently conditional on their raw ESPN projections and the selected group-level calibrated outcome distributions. Do not add quarterback–receiver stacks, teammates/opponents, shared game-script shocks, team-level random factors, player-level latent factors, or across-week injury/usage persistence in V1. This independence assumption governs performance draws, not the deterministic dependence of team outcomes, standings, waiver access and playoff qualification generated by the shared league simulation. Correlation and persistent player-state processes remain post-V1 research enhancements to assess using true joint historical player-week data, robust shrinkage, and impact on calibrated championship probabilities, especially for stacked rosters. Keep common random numbers for paired scenario variance reduction: sharing a random stream across *counterfactual scenarios* is not a model of correlation between different players within one season path.

V1 negative-score support (agreed): Do not impose an artificial zero floor on realized fantasy scores. Where this league's confirmed ESPN scoring configuration allows negative player-week outcomes (for example through turnovers or negative D/ST scoring), empirical scoring distributions and Monte Carlo samples must retain plausible negative values and respect the support, tails and scoring granularity validated in historical data. A positive ESPN projection governs pre-week availability but does not force a nonnegative realized score. Where a specific position/scoring mechanism cannot yield negative scores under verified league settings, its model should not create impossible negatives. Compare negative-outcome frequency and lower-tail calibration by position and projected point level; any fitted or resampled distribution must handle permissible signed observations without indiscriminate clamping or rounding. Include tests confirming negative sampled scores survive roster scoring, matchup totals, standings and postseason logic. ### 4.7 Historical validation limitations

Observed fantasy outcomes can test the distribution of projection errors where genuine pregame projections are available. Prospective stored snapshots provide cleaner evidence over time but are initially collected only during explicit refresh. Replaying a new model on old ESPN snapshots can compare computational/model approaches but does not independently validate ESPN’s original forecasts or prove optimal transaction decisions. Preserve dates, model versions and train/test separation; report if historical forecast data is too sparse or post-hoc revised for strong calibration claims.

## 5. Weekly league simulation mechanics

### 5.1 Timeline of each simulated future week

Start from the per-path roster ownership, current cumulative standings, waiver priority and free-agent state. Identify positions that CANNOT be legally filled by any AVAILABLE rostered player. Process only the essential acquisitions required to fill these holes before final lineup selection. After any permitted add/drop, optimize a legal lineup using ESPN weekly projections; freeze that lineup; then draw fantasy outcomes and resolve scheduled matchups. Update wins/losses, points for, relevant tiebreakers, waiver order and bracket advancement for later weeks. Repeat through the remaining regular season and full postseason.

### 5.2 Projected-optimal starting lineup

Maximize total ESPN projected points across all mandatory starter and flex slots, honoring player eligibility, all relevant league slot constraints and the one-player-one-slot requirement. Bench/eligible flex allocation must be globally optimal, not an error-prone greedy slot-by-slot choice. Select starters before seeing stochastic outcomes. If no roster/free-agent combination can fill a mandatory slot, leave it empty for a zero contribution in that week and log it; do not invent a synthetic player. Future manager attentiveness, mistaken starts and discretionary streaming are out of scope for the V1 baseline.

M2.1 implements this contract as a deterministic exact dynamic program over the configured starter-slot instances and eligible rostered players. Only configured `STARTER` capacity creates destinations; bench, reserve/IR, and observed-but-unconfigured eligibility categories do not. A candidate must be on the selected modeled roster, outside reserve, eligible for the destination, covered by the requested canonical player-week, and have `effective_projection > 0`. Exact stored float values are compared without a tolerance. Objective ties prefer more filled positions and then the lexicographically stable assignment formed from configured slot ID, within-slot position index, and signed player ID. If a complete lineup is impossible, the optimizer returns the best feasible partial assignment, explicit deterministic vacancies and shortage reasons, with zero contribution from each vacancy. It accepts a separately validated complete roster state for later path-dependent simulation without mutating the source snapshot. Distribution-aware lineup objectives or tie decisions remain a deferred enhancement and must account for matchup and joint outcome context; they are not part of M2.1.

### 5.3 Essential waiver claims

An essential waiver action occurs ONLY when the upcoming projected legal starting lineup has at least one mandatory vacancy that cannot be filled by any available eligible player already rostered. A merely suboptimal but legal existing player does not justify an upgrade. Candidate additions come from actual available unowned ESPN players in the shared simulation-path pool, respecting league mechanics. A transaction is eligible only when it is necessary and improves the current-week projected legal lineup relative to leaving the vacancy unresolved. Among legal eligible add/drop transactions, select the transaction producing the highest unified remaining-season V(R), even when that choice sacrifices some current-week projected points relative to another eligible transaction. This supersedes the earlier current-week-gain-only ranking; behavior remains reactive because no transaction occurs without the current vacancy trigger.

Process priority according to the actual ESPN league rules. For the verified current traditional weekly-reset system, freeze the applicable weekly priority before the transaction window and move each successful waiver claimant to the last position dynamically. Each manager may obtain at most one essential player per pass through priority order; repeat passes for additional unfillable slots until no further legal acquisitions are possible. Re-evaluate roster/availability/pool after each action. All remaining managers, including teams eliminated from title contention, can participate when consolation play continues.

Process waiver claims first and immediate free agents second inside one pregame transaction window after relevant waiver processing. Immediate free agents execute in reverse order of the initial frozen weekly waiver priority. This reverse-priority policy is an approved but uncalibrated and replaceable V1 manager-behavior assumption, not an ESPN rule: its provisional rationale is that better-performing managers may be more attentive to immediate opportunities, but that relationship has not been statistically validated. Preserve the distinction in outputs. Compare alternative ordering policies in future research and, where feasible, estimate manager-specific activity from historical transaction evidence.

The clear deterministic reference uses exact complete-cycle current-week lookahead over the small legal claim/free-agent allocation universe. Shared player allocation must be eligibility-aware rather than treating positional replacement pools independently. The operational engine retains one shared ownership state, transaction ledger, waiver priority, and dropped-player holding/reentry state across weeks. Verified ESPN transaction rules govern legality; V(R), essential-only behavior, and manager-processing order are separately labeled modeled policies.

### 5.4 Roster cuts and evolving shared player pool

Players added via essential waivers are genuinely owned and persist after a bye ends until some later eligible roster transaction. When full, the manager drops a player chosen for minimal replacement-adjusted marginal roster value, accounting for starter use, positional scarcity, future bye/waiver replaceability and roster depth; never drop a player essential to that week’s still-viable projected lineup. This needs an efficient approximation, not nested complete season Monte Carlo for every possible drop. The precise unified V(R) is a research task covered in Section 7.

Acquired players leave the pool immediately, and no player has duplicate ownership. Players dropped during one weekly waiver cycle are NOT eligible for new claims in that same cycle; they can re-enter eligible free agency after the league-specific delay in a later week. This differs deliberately from hypothetical initial scenario completion in Section 8, where all automatic drops are reconciled into the pool before all adds.

IR management is reactive, never proactive. A player currently occupying a configured reserve/IR slot cannot enter a starting lineup directly. An explicit legal modeled activation must first move the player to an ordinary occupied slot, including any required capacity adjustment. M2.2 enforces only supplied modeled occupancy and does not activate players.

Future M2.3 supports two explicit simulation policies. Under `projection_based`, a positive future effective projection is the approved modeled recovery proxy for both activation when necessary and whether a lingering IR occupant creates an acquisition-blocking invalid-roster issue. Under `strict`, projections alone establish neither recovery nor an ineligible IR condition; separately verified evidence is required. Both policies react only when lineup or acquisition legality requires action. ESPN undroppable protection is an independent drop constraint: when the league enables the list, a confirmed protected player cannot be dropped and an unknown applicable player status cannot be assumed droppable; when disabled, that designation does not prohibit a drop under this rule. Other locks and restrictions remain independent.

Unified V(R) values additions and removals over all remaining weeks with equal temporal weight, conservative competition-adjusted replacement access, and shared eligibility-aware allocation. It may approximate future access but may not nest Monte Carlo inside roster valuation. The M2.3c production reference retains distinct consumed-player allocations even when they fill the same vacancy mask; only a proved admissible projection upper bound may prune after complete opposing coverage exists. No nested full Monte Carlo for each roster move inside every simulated waiver step. Exact computational approximation and replacement levels remain OPEN research questions requiring measured decision quality/performance tests; temporal weights are fixed equally in V1.

Do not introduce unsupported speculative future claims into the V1 baseline: V(R)'s approximation forecasts the opportunity cost of depth, while the actual season simulator still executes only its specified essential path-dependent waiver events. Exact future demand model, choice of replacement tier and calibration remain open research items.

## 8. Generalized what-if evaluation and scenario completion

### 8.1 Scope and input semantics

The core counterfactual evaluator accepts coherent roster-state modifications affecting any manager(s), not exclusively trade-for-trade exchanges. Manual instructions can include explicit player transfers between teams, acquiring currently unrostered ESPN players, and dropping existing players. V1 changes take effect just before the first simulated week; no midseason scheduled actions or overriding league lineup-size rules. Track ownership and prevent invented players. Scenario inputs may be temporarily overfilled/underfilled while being specified, but every actual simulated starting state must be structurally achievable, full and legal.

The user’s explicit moves are intentional binding constraints for scenario completion even when analytically unwise. The scenario is not permission to duplicate ownership or ignore roster capacity.

### 8.2 Deterministic initial completion algorithm

Stage 1: Apply explicit transfers, acquisitions and removals, reconcile source owners and the unrostered pool, and preserve user-directed pins. Stage 2: Across affected teams, make ALL necessary automatic roster-capacity drops before choosing incidental pickups; released players immediately become available to the shared hypothetical completion pool. Stage 3: Refill all roster vacancies up to normal capacity, using remaining-season V(R), in round-robin allocation ordered by current real ESPN waiver priority, at most one add per team per pass. This order is a deterministic fairness heuristic for hypothetical completion, NOT a claim that actual waiver acquisitions could occur exactly this way.

Any currently unrostered ESPN player can be chosen during initial hypothetical completion even if currently on real waiver lockdown, because the purpose is to create a coherent counterfactual state. Completion is computed ONCE before Monte Carlo simulation, not anew in each season path. At simulation start, explicit pins expire: a later simulated essential waiver event may legally drop the initially forced player. If no legal, full roster can be created while honoring pins, report infeasibility rather than silently changing the user’s chosen edits.

### 8.3 Counterfactual league comparison

Baseline and scenario use an IDENTICAL immutable ESPN source snapshot and the same player-week random shocks, keyed by stable player/week/path IDs, so estimated probability differences benefit from common random numbers. They may diverge in future roster acquisitions, waiver priority and lineups because those are genuine downstream consequences of the hypothetical. Rank candidate scenarios by EXPECTED improvement in championship probability, not by requiring statistical significance. For large screens, guard against multiple-comparison winner’s curse; use distinct scenario-difference precision targets from baseline absolute-probability targets and reserve further detailed sampling for promising finalists.

### 9.5 Independent trade feasibility, value and acceptability

Agreed separation: Evaluate trade outcomes and trade acceptance independently. Legal feasibility is an objective constraint from ESPN ownership, roster/transaction rules and trade settings; violations exclude a trade as actionable. Conditional transaction value is determined by the same league-wide paired Monte Carlo counterfactual, assuming the trade occurs, with championship-probability changes and uncertainty reported for ALL teams. Another manager's willingness to ACCEPT a proposed trade is a separate, uncertain analysis layer, never an input that modifies simulated player performances, underlying roster-state counterfactuals, random paths or full-league championship deltas. The same trade should have identical conditional Monte Carlo outcomes regardless of which manager proposes/views it, even if each side's estimated acceptance likelihood or preferred ranking differs.

Acceptance/sellability research may consider counterparty projected ESPN-point changes, expected starting-lineup/roster value V(R), positional needs/depth, observed manager behavior and other justified evidence, but must not equate 'beneficial to both sides' with guaranteed acceptance or assign fabricated probabilities. Start with transparent directional partner-value diagnostics and optional user filters, such as requiring a minimum projected benefit or limiting estimated harm to the other team; calibrated acceptance probabilities should only be offered when suitable historical or prospective evidence actually supports them. Keep the search objective (e.g. maximize a selected manager's championship gain), acceptance filters, and statistical evaluation precision as distinct configurable settings. In result displays and exports show the counterparty's conditional championship impact and any independently estimated acceptability information side by side, clearly labeled; do not multiply championship delta by a speculative acceptance rate as the default ranking or mislabel an unattractive trade as impossible. Future multi-transaction or waiver-execution logic can explicitly model probability of successful execution when justified, without contaminating the conditional outcome model.

Initial counterparty-attractiveness metric (agreed): Use the SAME fast, ESPN-projection-based, replacement-adjusted V(R) policy as elsewhere in the project to calculate each trade participant's marginal roster-value change, delta V(R) = V(R after proposed transaction and lawful roster completion) minus V(R before transaction). In particular, the receiving manager's delta V(R) provides the primary simple indicator of whether the offer improves their projected starting opportunities, considering lineup eligibility, byes, positional scarcity and foreseeable replacement access. Calculate it for both participants for consistency; also make raw ESPN projected-point or lineup-point differences available as optional transparent diagnostics, not competing primary valuation engines. A positive partner delta V(R) is NOT a quantified acceptance probability or guaranteed agreement, and a negative value does not conclusively imply rejection. Keep this attractiveness screen separately configurable from legal trade feasibility, the requester's ranking objective and league-wide paired championship-probability Monte Carlo outcomes. Do not silently discard every trade with a negative counterparty delta unless the user explicitly enables that filter. More sophisticated behavior/acceptance inference is deferred pending evidence; do not fabricate probabilities.

Counterparty-attractiveness filter default (confirmed): Do NOT automatically reject or prune a legally feasible proposed trade simply because the other participant has negative delta V(R) or an unfavorable projected-point comparison. By default, evaluate trade potential independently of the likelihood that an offer will be accepted; show both participants' delta V(R) as informative diagnostics. Users may explicitly enable a configurable minimum counterparty delta V(R), nonnegative partner-benefit requirement or corresponding sort/filter, either during candidate screening or while exploring simulated results. Such optional sellability assumptions must be disclosed, must never be confused with league legality, and must not alter the conditional full-league Monte Carlo results for a transaction. Maintain visibility of asymmetric trades when those filters are off; real managers can have preferences not captured by V(R).

Automatic final-state equivalence and simulation reuse (confirmed): AFTER applying all explicit transactions, vacancy-event-prioritized roster completion, and final legality checks, construct a deterministic canonical fingerprint of the COMPLETE simulation-relevant starting league state, not merely the requesting manager's roster or the original traded player list. Include all teams' player ownership/rosters and other effective state inputs that could change future league simulation (such as current shared unrostered pool, required roster/eligibility settings, standings/waiver state, league rule configuration and the exact pinned input snapshot/model configuration). Two distinct legal transaction paths that produce truly identical fingerprints MUST map to the SAME scenario evaluation ID and reuse the SAME Monte Carlo result, path counts, CIs and per-team deltas; do not independently simulate such paths or rely on same random seed alone to approximately match. Preserve all original trade-chain descriptions, execution/acceptability diagnostics and path-specific metadata as separate records linked many-to-one to the evaluated state. Equivalence is assessed only after completion: differing vacancy priorities or drops may yield different final rosters even if the explicit trade ownership matches. Canonical keys must use stable IDs, order-independent sorting where appropriate, exact model-relevant values (no lossy rounding), and version/snapshot scoping so different settings are never mistakenly merged. A fingerprint collision or inconsistent state-to-result linkage is an invariant violation that must fail loudly. Reuse evaluations only inside the relevant current search/run or approved checkpoint lifecycle; this does not imply retaining stale results across projection updates. Treat this as a mandatory exact optimization compatible with exhaustive enumeration, never candidate pruning. Test that equivalent paths yield byte-for-byte identical displayed league-wide probabilities and confidence intervals and that genuinely different simulation-relevant states are not accidentally merged.

Execution scheduling: efficiency without enumeration-order bias (confirmed): Permit overlapping candidate generation, roster completion/screening, and early paired Monte Carlo evaluation using bounded independent worker queues when benchmarks show a benefit; retain a sequential reference/debug mode. Processing order, batch size, worker count, candidate IDs, and CPU scheduling MUST NOT determine which legal candidates qualify, which canonical final states are deemed equal, or their simulation results/uncertainty for the same prescribed paths. If Monte Carlo starts before enumeration finishes, apply an equivalent initial evaluation policy to every eligible unique final state, using path-indexed common random numbers. Do not award earlier-generated candidates extra precision merely due to arrival time. Adaptive finalist selection or unequal precision allocation should occur only after the eligible universe is known, or through an independently validated statistically order-neutral policy; final refinement and all-team confidence-interval reporting must obey the previously agreed rules. If a search ends because of a time/path/candidate cap before full coverage, state explicitly which stages are incomplete, identify unequal evaluation exposure, and never present the best-so-far candidate as a confirmed global best of the feasible universe. Choose sequential versus pipelined execution based on measured performance, not an arbitrary hardcoded assumption. Compare results and candidate coverage across different generation orders, batch sizes, worker counts and scheduling modes; fail fast on unexpected differences. ## 10. Monte Carlo numerical engine, efficiency and operating modes

### 10.1 Batch convergence and statistical precision

Simulate full league seasons in reproducible batches rather than a fixed arbitrary number of paths. For each of 14 manager championship probabilities, default to a 95% confidence interval half-width ≤0.5 percentage points, subject to a minimum simulation sample size and maximum resource/safety budget. Expose confidence level and precision target separately. Use numerically defensible intervals and validate sequential stopping/checking to avoid falsely narrow intervals. Note 14 marginal 95% intervals do not guarantee a joint 95% coverage probability.

For scenario comparisons, separately control the standard error/precision of P(championship | scenario) − P(championship | baseline), using paired samples rather than naively adding errors of independent simulations. Report actual path count, confidence method, achieved width, convergence status and estimates failing precision due to time/budget limits.

### 10.2 Fast vs detailed execution

Execution policy: Do not require two rigid simulation-precision modes. Users configure confidence level, target interval half-width, path caps and time budget independently, with editable convenient defaults for exploratory or detailed work. The same official Monte Carlo rules and outcome model always apply. Terminate at a user-selected hard cap when necessary; display interim results and clearly mark unmet targets. Adaptive run time is uncertain and should be estimated through an optional representative pre-run benchmark on current hardware, then refined from observed run progress. Choose default confidence and tolerance values after benchmarking and calibration.

Adjustable Monte Carlo controls (agreed): Expose statistical precision as meaningful numeric inputs rather than only opaque quick/balanced/precise categories. User selects confidence level (default 95%), target confidence-interval half-width in percentage points (rather than ambiguous full CI width), and maximum simulation paths and/or wall-clock time; optional minimum initial path batch and adaptive increment limits may remain advanced settings. Distinguish absolute baseline championship-probability precision across all teams from paired scenario-minus-baseline championship-probability delta precision; these are different estimands with different sampling variability and may need different tolerances. Clearly state which target governs a run, and show the estimated CI, current half-width, path count, confidence level and whether the target was reached for every result. Use defensible interval and sequential-stopping methods when sampling adaptively; do not assert a guaranteed CI before outcomes are observed. Keep optional convenience presets as editable collections of these numeric inputs. Running more trade candidates and obtaining tighter probability intervals are independent choices; benchmark cost will depend on both. A hard time or path ceiling may terminate runs before the requested precision is reached, in which case return labeled partial estimates rather than claiming convergence.

League-wide precision default (latest decision): Every run computes championship probability outcomes for every team. Baseline simulations target the selected confidence-interval precision for all teams' absolute championship probabilities. Trade, waiver, and other scenario comparisons target precision for all teams' paired championship-probability changes versus the same baseline. The trade's participating teams or the selected viewer must not alter that default stopping rule. Rankings may focus on the user's benefit, but simulation outputs and stopping are league-wide. Identical scenarios, model settings, random seeds, and simulation budgets must reproduce the same full-league results regardless of viewing perspective. Allow explicit user-adjustable confidence level, target CI half-width, path limits, and runtime caps. Report achieved CI and convergence for each team, including unfinished runs. Adaptive path counts may differ across different transactions because uncertainty differs; do not imply every trade needs the same simulation count. Benchmark the computational cost and retain proper paired-path methods and finalist-selection safeguards.

Progressive candidate refinement (agreed): For automated searches, every candidate that passes the chosen deterministic screening receives an initial paired Monte Carlo evaluation producing the FULL league-wide vector of championship probabilities and paired deltas. Allocate subsequent simulation batches preferentially to promising or ambiguous candidate transactions, not to selected managers within a transaction. At every stage, sample and report outcomes for all teams on the same scenario paths. Finalists designated as fully evaluated must satisfy the configured confidence-interval half-width for ALL teams' paired championship-probability deltas (or explicitly disclose an unmet resource cap). Preliminary candidates can be ranked with wider intervals but must be labeled provisional, with sample counts and uncertainty. Use uncertainty-aware elimination or conservative retention of plausible contenders; do not discard a candidate merely because its noisy early point estimate is slightly worse. When practical, use common path IDs across candidate scenarios and baseline to support paired comparisons; protect final rankings against selection bias from screening many candidates by reevaluating finalists with appropriate controlled or fresh samples. A request for exhaustive coverage means every feasible candidate in the bounded universe receives its promised initial MC evaluation, not necessarily final-target convergence for every candidate; an optional stronger all-candidates-fully-converged requirement can be offered with an explicit cost warning. Keep candidate-search completeness, preliminary-versus-final precision, and all-team convergence status separately visible.

Configurable hybrid finalist selection (agreed): After each initial paired MC stage, retain at least a user-configured minimum number of candidate transactions based on the specified search ranking objective, plus additional candidates that remain statistically competitive with the currently best estimated candidate. Use an uncertainty-aware comparison of candidate-to-candidate expected improvement (ideally paired/common-path contrasts), accounting for variance and the risk of winner's curse when searching many possibilities; simple overlap of independently reported confidence intervals is not by itself a valid competitiveness test. A candidate with meaningful remaining probability of outperforming the leader should normally remain eligible for extra sampling. Expose configurable competitiveness criterion/tolerance, minimum finalists, optional maximum finalists, simulation batch budget and time cap; if limits force elimination of potentially competitive candidates, explicitly mark the result as budget-limited and the ranking as not fully resolved. Reassess competitiveness after new batches until selected finalists satisfy the existing all-league delta precision requirement, hit caps, or become clearly dominated. All comparisons and finalist choices may rank by a particular manager's championship gain, but every sampled path still evaluates all league teams and convergence for finalized candidates remains league-wide. Benchmark finalist recall against broad/full candidate evaluations; exact test statistic and thresholds remain empirical implementation decisions.

### 10.3 Determinism, shared shocks and parallel processing

Provide seeded, path-indexed deterministic random streams; results should be reproducible regardless of worker count or batch assignment. Match random outcomes for the same player-week-path across baseline and scenario; preserve realistic differences in downstream roster/waiver states. Support single-process debugging and parallel multi-core CPU execution in V1. Batch workers should share or efficiently initialize immutable input arrays, return incremental sufficient statistics, avoid high-volume serialization and aggregate with deterministic path accounting.

### 10.4 Hot-path optimization

Prefer compact integer player/team/position encodings, precomputed per-week projections/eligibility, NumPy vectorized calculations where suitable, and compiled numerical loops (e.g., Numba) for branch-heavy path-dependent weekly waivers and state updates if profiling justifies it. Avoid repeated pandas merges, DataFrames or Python objects per season path. Benchmark actual end-to-end structurally complete runs, memory, startup/JIT overhead and parallel scaling; keep a slower clear reference implementation to cross-check optimizations.

### 10.5 Progress, checkpoints and retention

Publish progressive batches with estimated league probabilities, CI/precision, sample counts, elapsed time and stop criterion. For detailed/long runs, periodically persist compact atomic checkpoints containing immutable input/model/settings identity, seed/path-ID ranges, processed batches and cumulative sufficient statistics. Recovery must neither lose nor double-count completed paths, and resumed results should agree with uninterrupted execution. Detailed checkpoints are cleaned after successful completion and abandoned ones expire under retention rules. Fast mode ordinarily restarts if interrupted. Operational recovery checkpoints are not a permanent general-purpose completed-scenario cache.

### 12.2 Practical development method

Each increment is a runnable vertical slice. Start with the clean repository and read-only ESPN diagnostic; once actual data is verified, create minimal canonical snapshot, then the complete league path, then upgrade its statistical and roster policies without changing external behavior or data contracts unnecessarily. At each checkpoint, write tests, benchmark end-to-end behavior, document assumptions and decide whether to promote the new algorithm. A toy fixed-roster unit test may support development but is never presented as the first full simulator product.

### 12.4 Primary risks and mitigation

R1 ESPN endpoints or historical forecasts unavailable/revised: verify raw fields and provenance; flag unsupported assumptions, do not quietly import third-party means. R2 waiver/bracket rule ambiguity: test actual ESPN league config and synthetic edge cases before official forecasting. R3 runtime explosion: compact compiled paths, parallel batches, selective logging, staged scenario screening. R4 weak roster value evidence: provisional policy with controlled comparisons and explicit limitations. R5 hindsight/model leakage: immutable as-of snapshots, versioned replays and sealed test periods. R6 document/UI scope growth: work from this single specification; no frontend or scheduled background collection until core evidence and product merit it.
